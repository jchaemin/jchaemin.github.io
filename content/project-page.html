In-Context Examples Suppress Scientific Knowledge Recall in LLMs

Chaemin Jang, Woojin Park, Hyeok Yun, Dongman Lee, Jihee Kim

KAIST · Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Models recall the right formula on their own. Show them a few examples of it, and they stop deriving. They start pattern-fitting instead.

The strategy shift

Validation

A causal check

Is this real, or just noisy chain-of-thought labels? A paired log-probability test on three open models says real.

Example

How a task looks

Example task from the economics domain, shown zero-shot and few-shot. What is the consumer surplus at the Cournot-Nash equilibrium?

Zero-shot: correct

Step 1: Identify Cournot model
  “Two firms, linear demand P = a − bQ”
Step 2: Derive equilibrium
  q* = (a−c)/3b = 20, Q* = 40
Step 3: Compute CS from the formula
  CS = ½(a−P*)Q* = 2(a−c)²/9b
     = 1600 ✓

Derived from the Cournot equilibrium

Few-shot: wrong

Step 1: Fit pattern from examples
  “CS scales roughly with market size”
Step 2: Apply fitted rule
  CS ≈ 1522 ... or 1688
  (varies by trial) ✕

Fitted heuristic, inconsistent across trials

Interpretation

Why recall is so easy to suppress

Swap “price” for a meaningless code word. Keep the same math. Zero-shot accuracy: 55–88% → 3–14%. The model isn't deriving the answer. It's recognizing keywords. That's why a few examples are enough to knock it off course.

Takeaways

What we find

  • Uniform direction. Every domain loses 6–20pp of formula-based reasoning, even where accuracy barely moves.
  • Strong priors lose most. Economics (88.3% prior) drops most; Geoscience (55.3% prior) looks best: a weak prior is just easy to beat, not improved on.
  • Recall is a keyword trigger. Swap the vocabulary, keep the math. Accuracy collapses to single digits.
