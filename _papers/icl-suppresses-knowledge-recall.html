Models recall the right formula on their own. Show them a few examples of it, and they stop deriving. They start pattern-fitting instead.

The Strategy Shift

Few-shot examples pull every domain toward pattern-fitting, whether or not it helps.

Same-size shift, opposite outcomes: Economics −15.7 accuracy pp, Geoscience +13.3 accuracy pp. Shown at 10-shot; the direction holds from k=1 through k=20.

A Causal Check

Is this real, or just noisy CoT labels? A paired log-probability test on three open models says real.

Probability mass moves toward the example answer and away from the knowledge answer, on the same input, every time.

How a Task Looks

One real trace, same question, Economics.

Zero-shot: correct
Cournot model: P = a − bQ
q* = (a−c)/3b,  Q* = 2(a−c)/3b
CS = ½(a−P*)Q* = 2(a−c)²/9b
   = 1481.5  ✓

derived from the Cournot equilibrium

10-shot: wrong
"CS scales ~ 0.5 × Q² / slope"
CS ≈ 0.5 × 66.7² / 1.5
   = 1482.1 ... or 1507.3  ✗

fitted heuristic, inconsistent across trials

Zero-shot derives it. 10-shot fits a rough rule that lands on a different wrong number each time.

Why Recall Is So Easy to Suppress

Swap "price" for a meaningless code word. Keep the same math. Zero-shot accuracy: 55–88% → 3–14%. The model isn't deriving the answer. It's recognizing keywords. That's why a few examples are enough to knock it off course.

What We Find

Uniform direction. Every domain loses 6–20pp of formula-based reasoning, even where accuracy barely moves.

Strong priors lose most. Economics (88.3% prior) drops most; Geoscience (55.3% prior) looks best: a weak prior is just easy to beat, not improved on.

Recall is a keyword trigger. Swap the vocabulary, keep the math. Accuracy collapses to single digits.
